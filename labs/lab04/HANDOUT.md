## Part A · The archive, 0:15 to 0:55

### The problem

A folder of DICOM files is not a tidy data structure. The hierarchy that matters

```
Patient  ->  Study  ->  Series  ->  Instance
```

is not in the folder names and not in the file names. It is in four
identifiers, and the only way to know what you have is to open every file and
read them.

| level | what it is | the identifier |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | a person | PatientID, within an issuer |
| Study | one visit to the scanner for one reason | StudyInstanceUID |
| Series | one acquisition, one set of parameters | SeriesInstanceUID |
| Instance | one image, or one report, or one object | SOPInstanceUID |

Those UIDs are globally unique and permanent. The same study copied to three
hospitals keeps the same StudyInstanceUID in all three. That is the property
the whole imaging world is built on, and it is also why a single UID collision
is serious in a way a duplicated row in a CSV is not.

### Task 5 — `check_patient_consistency`, in `src/checks.py`

Two questions, in this order.

**Does every study belong to exactly one patient?** Group by `study_uid` and

`patient_name` values, or two birth dates, or two sexes. This one is a note
rather than a blocker, and Part C is where you decide what it means.

### Task 6 — the rest of the findings

The other checks in `src/checks.py` are written for you. Run them all,

**A finding is not an error message.** It names the thing, says where it is,
and says what breaks if nobody acts on it. "SliceThickness is 3.0" is not a
finding. The ones written for you are the model; match them.

## Part B · Pixels and geometry, 0:55 to 1:35

### The three numbers

| | what it is | units |
|---|---|---|
| stored value | the integer in PixelData | none. 24 is not a quantity of anything |
| real value | stored × RescaleSlope + RescaleIntercept | HU for CT, by calibration |
| displayed value | the real value windowed, and inverted if MONOCHROME1 | none. For looking at |

Hounsfield units are defined by two fixed points: water is 0 and air is −1000.
Those are not measurements, they are the definition, and they are what makes a
CT number from a scanner in Heraklion comparable with one from Helsinki. It is
also what makes the error in this archive findable without reading a single
tag: measure the air in the corner of the image and see whether it is −1000.

### Task 1 — `to_real_values`, in `src/image.py`

Three lines. Both tags are optional, an absent slope is 1 and an absent
intercept is 0, so the arithmetic always works and is sometimes silently wrong.

### Task 2 — measure the air

For every CT series in the archive, take the middle slice, convert it, and
print `image.corner_mean`. One series will not read about −1000.

Find it by measurement first. Then go and look at its tags and see what is
missing. The order matters: in a real archive nobody hands you a list of which
series to be suspicious of, and the tag you would have to check is the one that
is not there.

How far off is it, exactly? The number is not a coincidence.

### Task 3 — `to_display`, in `src/image.py`

Four steps, in the order given in the docstring. Then show the same CT slice
three times, at lung (C −600 W 1500), mediastinum (C 50 W 400) and bone
(C 400 W 1800).

Same pixels. Same Hounsfield units. Three pictures, and anything outside the
window is gone. This is why a measurement is never taken off a displayed image.

### Task 4 — the radiograph

One image in this archive is `MONOCHROME1`. Display it both ways, with and
without honouring the photometric interpretation, and put the two side by side.

Then say which one a human would notice and which one a program would not.

### Task 5 — `check_slice_geometry`, in `src/checks.py`

For every series with three or more slices: sort by `position_z`, take the
differences between neighbours, and compare what you find against
`SpacingBetweenSlices` and `SliceThickness`.

Three different things can come out of this and they are not the same problem:

- the gaps are not all equal
- the gaps are equal and do not match the tag
- a gap is exactly zero

The third one is the one to look at hardest.

Thickness and spacing being different is not by itself an error. An
acquisition can be gapped or overlapping on purpose. It is an error the moment
something downstream multiplies an area by a thickness and calls it a volume.

### Task 6 — measure something

Take a CT slice, pick two points, and report the distance in millimetres using

`distance_mm` returns `None` for one of them. Do not work around it. Write down
what you would have to do to report a number for that series, and who would

## Part C · The archive against the cohort, 1:35 to 1:55

### The problem

`8c3793c8-230d-ee62-60bf-fe61dcadd0d1`. Neither identifier appears in the other
file. The only bridge is the demographics, and the demographics disagree.

This is Week 7 again with two differences: the population is nine patients, so
you can look at every pair; and the output is not a score, it is a decision
about each person that somebody has to sign.

### Task 1 — what you are matching on

```python
cohort = reconcile.load_cohort("../lab01/data/patients.csv")
```

Look at both before writing the matcher. DICOM `PersonName` is
`family^given^middle^prefix^suffix`; the CSV has `FIRST` and `LAST` columns.
Birth dates are `YYYYMMDD` in one and `YYYY-MM-DD` in the other. One of the
archive patients has two names.

### Task 2 — `match`, in `src/reconcile.py`

Four statuses: `exact`, `partial`, `ambiguous`, `none`. The docstring defines
them. Fill `best_cohort_id` even when you reject the match.
